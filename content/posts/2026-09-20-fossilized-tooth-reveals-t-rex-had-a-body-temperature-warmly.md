By analyzing chemical clues in a 66-million-year-old fossil, scientists have discovered that the Tyrannosaurus rex maintained a warm-blooded body temperature of about 97 degrees Fahrenheit. This fascinating finding helps explain how the massive predator stayed active and thrived in chilly northern climates.
