Researchers have discovered a way to shield brain tissue from Alzheimer's damage by regulating specific immune cells that attack neurons. In animal trials, this targeted therapy successfully preserved roughly 40 percent more tissue in key memory centers and improved cognitive function, opening a promising new avenue for treatment.
