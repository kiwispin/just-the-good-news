Scientists studying 380-million-year-old fossils have discovered that early land animals had surprisingly stiff necks, reshaping our understanding of evolutionary history. The findings suggest that necks originally developed to help early creatures propel themselves using their front limbs, offering a clearer picture of how life made its historic transition from water to land.
